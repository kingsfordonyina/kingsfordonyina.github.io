Still Echoing: Welfare Reform and the Noncognitive Skill Formation of the Next Generation

Job market paper, 2026 · JEL: I38, J13, J24, H53

How did welfare reform affect the children who grew up under it? The 1990s overhaul of U.S. cash assistance replaced Aid to Families with Dependent Children (AFDC) with Temporary Assistance for Needy Families (TANF), introducing work requirements, time limits, sanctions, and greater state discretion. Prior research has examined effects on adults and children's short-run outcomes, but less is known about whether early-life exposure shaped human capital as exposed cohorts entered young adulthood. I study noncognitive skills (NCS), an overlooked socioemotional dimension of human capital. Linking the PSID core survey to the Child Development Supplement and Transition into Adulthood Supplement, I measure exposure from conception through age five and estimate a triple-difference design exploiting variation across states, birth cohorts, and mothers' likelihood of being affected. I find that early-life exposure lowered young-adult NCS by 0.23 standard deviations among individuals born to likely affected mothers. The decline is concentrated in agreeableness and openness, largest for earliest exposure, and most detectable among girls and White children, though subgroup differences are imprecise. Mechanism results point less to income or employment and more to early caregiving conditions. The findings show that safety-net reforms can leave lasting marks on less visible dimensions of children's human capital.

Impact of social protection policies on inclusive growth in Sub-Saharan Africa: evidence from a bias-corrected dynamic panel

Onyina, K., & Baye, R. S. (2024). Cogent Economics & Finance, 12(1), 2409421.

Using a panel of 48 sub-Saharan African countries over 1990–2022 and the LSDVC estimator, we find that social protection policies significantly contribute to inclusive growth, consistent with a Rawlsian theory of justice.
